## Student-supported operating paths

Nicolas explicitly reaffirmed all three operating paths below. He confirmed that FY2027 growth accelerates to 35% before fading. He expects NVIDIA-supported capacity investment and demand for AI datacenter optics to support the initial ramp; growth then slows as the market and installed capacity mature. AGI is a possible upside hypothesis, not an added base-case cash flow. His broad observation about rapid AI adoption is anecdotal; it is not treated as a measured market fact or independent valuation evidence.

He expects manufacturing experience, process refinement and efficiency from recent capital investment to improve gross margin. AI-assisted manufacturing and labor efficiency are possible mechanisms; no quantified labor savings are inserted without company evidence. He expects capex to remain substantial in dollars, with spending intensity tapering once capacity supports the forecast demand. Lower capex is not itself revenue or income: it reduces investing cash outflow, while its effect on future capacity must be assessed.

## Valuation and funding assumptions proposed for final review

Nicolas's risk rationale, recorded October 19: COHR has greater concentration and capital-cycle exposure than a diversified S&P 500 holding. If customer demand weakens after capacity expansion, underutilization, inventory accumulation and debt service can pressure cash and potentially require refinancing or dilution. This supports a beta above one qualitatively; it does not measure beta at exactly 1.75. The filing identifies two customers at 20% and 12% of FY2026 sales without naming them in that disclosure. Datacenter & Communications accounts for about 74.1% of sales, but includes communications applications beyond AI; the filing does not establish that AI alone is 74.1% or a majority of total revenue. COHR's modeled September market equity is about USD 53.1bn; manufacturing capital intensity is not attributed simply to being a small company.

Nicolas also suggested AI-lab IPOs as a potential funding catalyst. This is kept as an unquantified upside hypothesis. Pre-cutoff sources do not establish two confirmed 2027 IPOs: Altman's September interview excluded 2026 without committing to a 2027 completion date, while September 14 reporting still described a potential 2026 Anthropic listing. IPO fundraising does not automatically produce COHR orders, and no unverified customer identity is inferred. No IPO-driven cash-flow increment or reduction in risk is inserted.

Source pointers for that correction: [Altman interview transcript, September 15](https://www.fortune.com/2026/09/15/sam-altman-openai-ai-safety-pause-trump-xi/); [Anthropic IPO reporting, September 14](https://www.axios.com/2026/09/14/anthropic-ipo-safety-openai). These are timing checks, not transaction commitments or confirmed COHR customer evidence.

## Bounds and causal scenarios

Growth ±5 pp and margin ±2 pp retain Nicolas's Lab 11 selections. Capex ±2 pp tests roughly the difference between recent expansion intensity and the modeled fade; it allows some values outside history and is not guidance. Inventory ±30 days tests a meaningful partial reversal/worsening of the year-end inventory build rather than instant return to the earlier historical average. Historical departures are disclosed, not represented as proven normality.

Low case combines growth −5 pp, margin −2 pp, capex −1 pp, WACC 13%, g 2%: reduced demand/pricing with management investment restraint. The restraint is a material condition. A separate low-demand, sticky-investment case with capex +1 pp is retained as a funding failure rather than silently tuned away.

High case combines growth +5 pp, margin +2 pp, capex −0.5 pp, inventory −15 days, WACC 10%, g 3.5%: stronger demand with better utilization and cash discipline. Dollar capex is still substantial; this case requires spare capacity and execution. These combinations are proposed scenarios, not student-endorsed probabilities or statistical intervals.
