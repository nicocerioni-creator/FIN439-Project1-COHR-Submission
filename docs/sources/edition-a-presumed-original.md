# Project 1 Edition A - Coherent Corp.

**Valuation date:** September 1, 2026\
**Company:** Coherent Corp.\
**Ticker:** COHR (NYSE)\
**Decision user:** The fund's investment committee\
**Decision:** Whether the fund should initiate an investment position in Coherent Corp.

## Initial thesis: Watch / defer

Do not initiate a position yet. Coherent reported strong revenue growth, a substantial earnings recovery, and particularly strong demand from AI-datacenter customers. However, the current evidence does not establish that the shares are attractively valued as of the valuation date. Fiscal 2026 operating cash flow also fell sharply while capital expenditures and inventory increased substantially. The company should remain on the watchlist until its market valuation, normalized cash-generation capacity, competitive position, and customer concentration are researched.

This is an initial research conclusion, not a final investment recommendation.

## Synthetic enterprise-to-equity bridge check

Equity value represents the market value attributable to common shareholders. Enterprise value approximates the value of the operating business attributable to all capital providers. It is not simply the amount paid to shareholders in an acquisition.

The bridge is:

> Enterprise value = Equity value + Debt + Preferred stock + Noncontrolling interests - Cash and cash-like investments

Using Coherent's June 30, 2026 balance-sheet amounts:

> Enterprise value = Equity value + \$3,222.224m debt + \$0 preferred stock + \$334.705m noncontrolling interests - \$1,162.018m cash - \$825.000m short-term investments

> **Enterprise value = Equity value + \$1,569.911 million**

The reverse bridge is:

> **Equity value = Enterprise value - \$1,569.911 million**

The arithmetic checks. A dated market capitalization or enterprise value is still required to calculate the other absolute value. This treatment assumes the full \$825 million of short-term investments is excess cash-like value and that no additional debt-like or non-operating adjustments are required.

## Consequential assumptions

- The relevant decision is whether to initiate a position for the fund, not merely whether Coherent passes a preliminary personal-investing screen.
- September 1, 2026 is the valuation date, but the initial evidence is primarily based on financial information through June 30, 2026.
- Short-term investments are treated as cash-like in the enterprise-value bridge.
- Reported fiscal 2026 earnings are not automatically assumed to represent normalized earnings because they include a \$124 million gain on the sale of a business and other potentially nonrecurring items.
- Negative fiscal 2026 free cash flow may reflect growth investment rather than permanent deterioration, but this must be demonstrated rather than assumed.
- AI-datacenter demand can remain an important growth driver, but recent growth is not assumed to continue at 40% indefinitely.
- Small- and medium-sized companies may offer greater growth potential, but smaller size does not by itself mean a company is inexpensive or lower risk.
- No P/E, PEG, price-to-sales, or enterprise-value multiple is treated as known until dated market data and a defensible growth estimate are obtained.

## Important unknowns

- COHR's share price, market capitalization, and enterprise value on September 1, 2026.
- Valuation multiples relative to appropriate photonics, optical-networking, semiconductor-component, and industrial-laser peers.
- A defensible normalized EPS and free-cash-flow estimate after adjusting for divestitures, financing items, and nonrecurring gains and charges.
- The expected return and timing of the company's large capacity investments.
- Whether the inventory increase reflects well-supported customer demand, deliberate capacity preparation, supply-chain protection, or potential excess stock.
- The durability of AI-datacenter demand, Coherent's market share, product differentiation, pricing power, and exposure to technology substitution.
- The identities, economics, and durability of the customers responsible for major revenue concentration.
- Management's medium-term capital-expenditure, working-capital, margin, and debt-reduction plans.
- Verified insider open-market purchases, excluding grants, option exercises, gifts, and other non-discretionary transactions.
- Key legal, geopolitical, export-control, supply-chain, currency, and customer-concentration risks.
- Whether prospective returns are attractive relative to the S&P 500, bonds, and comparable companies after considering downside risk.
